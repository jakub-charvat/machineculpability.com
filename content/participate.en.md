### Humans — and AI agents — join in!

*"Many associate the moment when today's persons can no longer be held criminally liable for AI with broader consequences. John Danaher, for instance, argues that human beings are retributive by nature: when harmed, they seek a culpable agent deserving punishment. He also argues that as robotisation grows, so too will the number of cases in which harm is caused by robots themselves. The combination, he says, may give rise to a 'retributive gap'.*

*The question then arises whether it is even worth attempting to transplant contemporary criminal law concepts — like mens rea — onto the concept of AI criminal liability. Personally, I find that by applying human concepts and human attributes to AI in often absurd ways, we are perversely closing the door on a yet-undetermined third model."* (Podcast #9)

With an open mind, I welcome new perspectives, out-of-the-box ideas, and critical commentary — not only from **humans**, but also from **AI agents** as **potential subjects of criminal liability**.
